Mutual funds and fixed deposits are two distinct investment options with their own advantages and features.

1. Mutual Funds (Diversification, Professional Management, Flexibility, Lower Minimum Investment):
- Diversification: A basket of stocks, bonds, or other securities.
- Professional Management: Fund managers make investment decisions based on the fund's objectives.
- Flexibility: Units can be bought or sold at NAV or market price.
- Lower Minimum Investment: Generally, mutual funds require lower minimum investments compared to individual stocks.

2. Fixed Deposits (Savings Account, Maturity Tenure, Fixed Interest Rate, Liquidation and Withdrawal):
- Savings Account: Offered by banks and financial institutions.
- Maturity Tenure: Ranging from 1 day to 5 years or more.
- Fixed Interest Rate: Guaranteed based on initial deposit and agreed interest rate.
- Liquidation and Withdrawal: Possible after maturity without penalty, except for tax implications.
- Savings Account: Generally, fixed deposits have higher interest rates compared to traditional savings accounts. However, the fixed deposit option locks the funds for a specific period, whereas savings accounts offer more liquidity.
